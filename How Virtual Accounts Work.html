How virtual accounts work

Illustrated rules for paying by virtual account (VA) in Indonesia, written for the Dynamic Island payment card: the OS captures a VA from a shopping app, and a tap sends the user to their bank app to pay.

Example numbers and amounts are made up. Each rule matches a row in the “General rules VA” sheet.

01What a VA number is made of

A company code from the bank, then a number the company chooses. The bank itself is not inside the digits.

What happens

  • The prefix points to a merchant or a payment gateway, not always to the app.
  • The same digits mean nothing at another bank.

For the island

  • Capture the bank name together with the number.
  • Detect a 10–23 digit number next to words like “Virtual Account”, “Kode Bayar” or a bank name. Don't rely on one fixed length.

02A VA is not a normal bank account

A normal account belongs to a person. A VA is a label that lets the bank match a payment to one bill, then pass the money to the company's real account.

What happens

  • The bank shows the name tied to the VA (merchant, gateway or account holder) before you confirm.
  • Many apps collect through gateways like Xendit or Midtrans, so the name may be the gateway's.

For the island

  • Treat a VA as “pay this bill”, never as a payee to save.
  • Show the expected merchant name so the user can match it on the bank screen.

03Fixed VA vs non-fixed VA

A fixed VA stays the same for one person. A non-fixed VA is new for every order.

What happens

  • Fixed: users can pay into it again and again.
  • Non-fixed: once the order is paid or expired, the number is done.

For the island

  • Fixed: no countdown; can be remembered for repeat top-ups if the user agrees.
  • Non-fixed: one-time card; remove after paid or expired, never save.

04Single-use VAs, and why old numbers are dangerous

A single-use VA closes after one payment. Later, the same number can be issued to someone else's order.

What happens

  • Single-use: one payment, then inactive.
  • Multiple-use: many payments until the expiry date.

For the island

  • Never offer a pay button for a dynamic VA from history.
  • Delete the card once paid or expired.

05Closed amount vs open amount: what happens after entering the VA

Closed amount means the exact bill and nothing else. Open amount means the user picks how much.

What happens

  • Closed: exact amount down to the last digit; anything else fails.
  • Open: any amount above the minimum is accepted.
  • Fees (top-up fee, interbank fee) can sit on top.

For the island

  • Closed: capture the full amount, last 3 digits included; show it large with tap-to-copy.
  • Open: show “you choose the amount”; never invent one.

06Paying from the same bank vs another bank

Same bank is the smooth path. Another bank works only if the merchant's VA accepts it, and can be slower.

What happens

  • Gateways recommend paying a BNI VA from BNI, a BCA VA from BCA, and so on.
  • From another bank the user types the amount, adds fees and waits longer.

For the island

  • Default the bank button to the VA's own bank if that app is installed.
  • If the user picks a different bank, warn that it may fail and suggest changing the VA bank in the shopping app.

07Expiry: the money must arrive before the deadline

The merchant sets the deadline. What counts is when the money arrives, not when the user pressed send.

What happens

  • After expiry the VA can't be paid or revived; the user must check out again.
  • Interbank delays can push a payment past the deadline.

For the island

  • Count down from the deadline read on screen; if none is captured, say “check deadline in app”, never assume 24 hours.
  • Warn when time is short and the user picked another bank.
  • A new VA from the same app replaces the old card.

08Confirmation is automatic, but the OS can't see it

The bank tells the merchant in real time. The phone only learns about it when the shopping app updates or sends a notification.

What happens

  • Bank → gateway → merchant happens without the user.
  • The shopping app shows the new status.

For the island

  • When the user returns from the bank app, switch to “Waiting for confirmation”.
  • Mark as paid only from the shopping app's notification or screen, never by guessing.

09Two ways money goes to the wrong place

A misread digit, or a VA that didn't come from a real shopping app.

What happens

  • A valid but wrong VA is accepted by the bank; getting the money back is slow.
  • Scammers rely on people not checking the receiver's name.

For the island

  • Show the number in groups of 4 and remind the user to check the name.
  • Auto-capture only from an approved list of shopping apps; label the source app.

10Putting it together: the island payment journey

What the user sees, step by step, given the rules above and no bank partnership yet.

What the island removes

  • Switching back to the shopping app to re-read the number, amount and deadline.
  • Losing track of the deadline.

What it can't remove (yet)

  • Pasting into the bank app, unless banks agree to pre-fill.
  • Confirming payment by itself; that comes from the shopping app.

Not drawn here, but in the sheet: amount limits, Bank Indonesia's SNAP API standard, screen-capture blocking in some apps, and keeping phone-number VAs on the device only.
